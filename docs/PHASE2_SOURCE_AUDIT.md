## Corrections to the submitted research

1. **Timing citation accusation withdrawn.** Deisenhammer's Results distinguish decision-to-attempt (74.4% within ten minutes) from first-current-thought-to-attempt (47.6%). Paashaus's citation of the former is not an error on that basis. Separate results from selected survivor samples cannot establish a universal rate. See S03–S04. No universal replacement statistic is proposed.
2. **2023/2026 conflation corrected.** The multi-author Grossman critique is a 2026 commentary (S11). The separate 2023 single-author paper has DOI [10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108589](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108589); only its bibliographic identity was verified in this follow-up, not its full argument. Author count is not an evidence-quality measure.
3. **Outcomes separated.** The ideation finding belongs to Nuij (S14), not Stanley's measured cohort outcomes (S05). A non-significant result is not proof of no possible effect.
4. **Overclaims removed.** The blanket statement that all 15 claims are false/conflicting is not warranted. One primary category per entry now yields 8 narrower-support, 3 theoretical-framing, and 4 unresolved exact assertions. Zero exact support established in this bounded recheck is not proof of universal absence of evidence. Guidelines about communication do not experimentally falsify a biological theory.
5. **Review scope repaired.** Three numbered sessions plus the Crisis Package leaves **73**, not 72, numbered sessions outside scope. Unsupported references to nonexistent repository files, archive-play counts, and unrelated photograph/platform work were removed. Licensing review belongs to Gate 2, not Gate 6. The moral-injury sentence explicitly labels its interpretation as WRH's; its evidence gap does not establish a concealed attribution to Joiner.

## Inspected source catalogue

Publication dates below are those of the cited work; web pages without a reliable publication date are marked undated. Source IDs are stable within this audit. Reviews and author expositions are distinguished from original experiments; none is treated as independent validation of the theory it presents.

### S01

**VA/DoD (2024). Assessment and Management of Patients at Risk for Suicide, version 3.0, April 2024.** [Official index](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/srb/) · [full guideline](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/srb/VADOD-CPG-Suicide-Risk-Full-CPG-2024_Final_508.pdf) · [pocket guide](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/srb/VADOD-CPG-Suicide-Risk-Pocket-Card-2024_Final_508.pdf).

**Inspection:** Official index, pocket guide, targeted full-guideline recommendations (7 and 21). Index currently lists the 2024 version; no later guideline was established by this check. Recommendation 7 is neither for nor against safety/crisis-response planning for the specified attempt-reduction outcome; Recommendation 21 is weak-for reducing access to lethal means. This is clinical guidance, not WRH validation or a prohibition on safety planning. Scope and recommendation strength matter.

### S02

**Suicide Prevention Resource Center (undated). Safe and Effective Messaging and Reporting.** [Official page](https://sprc.org/keys-to-success/safe-and-effective-messaging-and-reporting/).

**Inspection:** Core guidance text, Reporting on Suicide and Developing Suicide Prevention Messages sections. Professional guidance favors hopeful/help-seeking communication and cautions about romanticizing death. It does not experimentally test Session 20 or establish the effect of its exercise. The legacy Safe and Effective Messaging handout cited in the submitted patch could not be reliably retrieved during this recheck; its exact quotation is not carried forward as independently verified support.

### S03

**Paashaus, L., et al. (2021). From decision to action: Suicidal history and time between decision to die and actual suicide attempt. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy, 28(6), 1427–1434.** [DOI/full text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cpp.2580).

**Inspection:** Full-text source; methods, Results §3.1, and timing discussion inspected. Retrospective interviews with 118 inpatients after an attempt. Results are sample-specific, affected by survivor/selection/recall limitations. See register ER-007; do not infer a universal rate or an individual timetable.

### S04

**Deisenhammer, E. A., et al. (2009). The Duration of the Suicidal Process: How Much Time Is Left for Intervention Between Consideration and Accomplishment of a Suicide Attempt? Journal of Clinical Psychiatry, 70(1), 19–24.** DOI [10.4088/JCP.07m03904](https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.07m03904) · [article copy](https://www.saferhomescollaborative.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Deisenhammer-2009-Duration-of-suicidal-process61420.pdf).

**Inspection:** Original paper copy; Methods/Results, printed pages 20–21. Retrospective sample of 82 Austrian patients. Timing anchors differ; population generalization is limited. See correction 1 and ER-007.

### S05

**Stanley, B., et al. (2018). Comparison of the Safety Planning Intervention With Follow-up vs Usual Care of Suicidal Patients Treated in the Emergency Department. JAMA Psychiatry, 75(9), 894–900.** DOI [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.1776](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.1776) · [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6142908/).

**Inspection:** Full-text source; design, abstract/results, outcomes, and discussion inspected. Non-randomized comparison of 1,640 adults at nine VA emergency departments. Combined SPI plus follow-up was associated with fewer recorded suicidal behaviors and greater treatment engagement; confounding and outcome ascertainment limit causal inference. It did not measure an ideation outcome demonstrating no effect and did not test WRH's transfer workflow. Do not convert a combination-intervention association into validation of a five-step handoff.

### S06

**Lieberman, M. D., et al. (2007). Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity in response to affective stimuli. Psychological Science, 18(5), 421–428.** DOI [10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x) · [journal-society abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x/).

**Inspection:** Abstract only. Image-labeling fMRI experiment reports amygdala and prefrontal differences. It does not establish a one-second oxygen effect, generalize directly to panic/crisis, or validate WRH. No full-text-only details are relied upon here.

### S07

**Kircanski, K., Lieberman, M. D., & Craske, M. G. (2012). Feelings into words: Contributions of language to exposure therapy. Psychological Science, 23(10), 1086–1091.** [Full text / PMID 22902568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721564/).

**Inspection:** Full-text source; abstract, Methods, and Discussion. Randomized experiment involving 88 spider-fearful participants. A follow-up skin-conductance difference did not coincide with a between-group self-reported fear benefit. Population, exposure procedure, follow-up timing, and outcome distinctions limit translation to WRH or suicidal crisis. This does not establish a universal labeling benefit or prove labeling ineffective.

### S08

**Ribeiro, J. D., et al. (2016; online 2015). Self-injurious thoughts and behaviors as risk factors for future suicide ideation, attempts, and death: A meta-analysis of longitudinal studies. Psychological Medicine, 46(2), 225–236.** [DOI 10.1017/S0033291715001804](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291715001804) · [publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/B035CF0E2B6FC95ECB523171CF704D21/S0033291715001804a.pdf/self-injurious-thoughts-and-behaviors-as-risk-factors-for-future-suicide-ideation-attempts-and-death-a-meta-analysis-of-longitudinal-studies.pdf).

**Inspection:** Abstract, NSSI results, Table 2, and discussion of prediction. Meta-analysis of 172 longitudinal studies with heterogeneous populations/intervals. NSSI association with later attempts and weak individual diagnostic accuracy are distinct findings; “eight prediction cases” does not mean eight participants. It does not establish the full WRH statement about recency, a universal function of NSSI, or a learned-pain nervous-system mechanism.

### S09

**Van Orden, K. A., et al. (2010). The interpersonal theory of suicide. Psychological Review, 117(2), 575–600.** DOI [10.1037/a0018697](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018697) · [original article copy](https://coping.us/images/IPT2010_The_Interpersonal_Theory_of_Suicide.pdf).

**Inspection:** Full-text source; opening model and hypothesis sections, including hopelessness. Theory/review, not a prospective validation trial of WRH. Distinguishes desire from capability and proposes hypotheses involving belongingness, burdensomeness, and hopelessness. It does not establish a deterministic rule or the separate WRH moral-injury interpretation. Empirical tests and moral-injury sources require separate review.

### S10

**Roelofs, K. (2017). Freeze for action: Neurobiological mechanisms in animal and human freezing. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 372, 20160206.** DOI [10.1098/rstb.2016.0206](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0206) · [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28242739/).

**Inspection:** Abstract only, successfully retrieved. Comparative review describes freezing as action-preparatory and discusses animal/human circuitry with explicit limits on human understanding. This is not a direct test of human dissociation, collapse, or rodent death-feigning as one mechanism.

### S11

**Grossman, P., et al. (2026). Why the polyvagal theory is untenable: An international expert evaluation of the polyvagal theory and commentary upon Porges, S.W. (2025). Clinical Neuropsychiatry, 23(1), 100–112.** [DOI 10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260110 / publisher](https://doi.org/10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260110).

**Inspection:** Publisher metadata/abstract and downloadable original PDF: opening commentary and selected physiological discussion. Critical expert commentary, not a new WRH trial. Documents substantial physiological/evolutionary criticism; the authors' conclusions are not treated as an experiment proving every psychological claim false. Distinct from Grossman's single-author 2023 article.

### S12

**Porges, S. W. (2025). Polyvagal Theory: Current Status, Clinical Applications, and Future Directions. Clinical Neuropsychiatry, 22(3), 169–184.** [DOI 10.36131/cnfioritieditore20250301 / publisher](https://www.clinicalneuropsychiatry.org/download/polyvagal-theory-current-status-clinical-applications-and-future-directions/).

**Inspection:** Publisher abstract/overview and bibliographic record only; not the full article. Author exposition includes neuroception and presents a proposed framework, not independent confirmation of itself. Publisher notes a February 2026 update adding references; reviewer should identify the version examined. This does not verify the wording of Porges's original 2004 article or claims about an individual brain.

### S13

**Zaccaro, A., et al. (2018). How Breath-Control Can Change Your Life: A Systematic Review on Psycho-Physiological Correlates of Slow Breathing. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 12, 353.** [DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353 / full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353/full).

**Inspection:** Abstract, eligibility criteria, selected findings, and Discussion. Review of 15 studies focused on healthy subjects. Associations involving breathing, physiological measures, and psychological outcomes do not validate the WRH RGP combination, mandatory use, or safety/effectiveness in acute distress. Breathing rate alone does not establish an exhale-specific effect.

### S14

**Nuij, C., et al. (2021). Safety planning-type interventions for suicide prevention: Meta-analysis. British Journal of Psychiatry, 219(2), 419–426.** DOI [10.1192/bjp.2021.50](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.2021.50) · [publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0007125021000507).

**Inspection:** Abstract, study table, Results, and Discussion/limitations. Six studies, heterogeneous designs/interventions; pooled behavioral risk ratio 0.57, no significant ideation result. Lack of significance does not establish no possible ideation effect. It does not validate WRH's transfer procedure or establish a result in every population. Read alongside the differently scoped guideline recommendation in S01.

### S15

**Zhu, X., et al. (2018). Exposure-based therapy changes amygdala and hippocampus resting-state functional connectivity in patients with posttraumatic stress disorder. Depression and Anxiety, 35(10), 974–984.** [DOI 10.1002/da.22816 / publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/da.22816).

**Inspection:** Publisher abstract, including design/results only. Twenty-four PTSD patients and 26 trauma-exposed controls; patients were rescanned after prolonged exposure, controls after waiting. Findings are treatment-associated group connectivity changes, not proof of an individual causal history, guaranteed recovery, or complete neural normalization.
