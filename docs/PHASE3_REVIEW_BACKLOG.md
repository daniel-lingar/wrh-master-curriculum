# WRH Phase 3 — Prioritized Review Backlog

> **UNAPPROVED TRIAGE — NOT FOR DELIVERY.** This backlog orders the Phase 3 claim inventory for qualified independent review. Priorities are inventory judgments made by AI-assisted reading; they are not evidence findings, not clinical decisions, and not approvals. No session script, participant material, crisis procedure, or operational instruction is changed by this document. Reviewer and approval fields for every item remain PENDING.

**Source:** [EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md](EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md) entries WRH-ER-016–WRH-ER-797 (782 entries, 977 verified entry-location records), with producer-reported reading coverage recorded in [PHASE3_READING_LEDGER.md](PHASE3_READING_LEDGER.md). Baseline `9ee354b42f622dcdc789f017ca8dc189bb649f0c`.

## Priority framework

Each entry's priority weighs four factors, in this order:

1. **Consequences of misunderstanding** — could a participant or facilitator act on this claim in a way that affects safety, health, or legal exposure if the claim is wrong?
2. **Wording** — is the claim stated universally, deterministically, or as a deliver-verbatim instruction, rather than as one person's experience or an attributed theory?
3. **Likely audience** — claims delivered to participants in acute distress, or instructing facilitators in crisis contexts, outrank the same claim in background narration.
4. **Relationship to existing blockers** — claims that extend, restate, or operationalize the BLOCKING items WRH-ER-001–004 (Session 20 crisis framing) and the safety-critical items ER-011–012 (Crisis Package) inherit their urgency.

- **P1** — Safety instructions; crisis/suicide-adjacent content; deterministic or permanence claims stated as fact; promised treatment effects; diagnostic reclassifications delivered to participants; institutional/certification claims.
- **P2** — Mechanism and causal claims; numerical claims; recovery/outcome predictions; theory presented without attribution.
- **P3** — Other empirical assertions, including pedagogical framings with empirical content.

Counts: **P1 = 255 entries, P2 = 445, P3 = 82.** Tag totals across the inventory (an entry may carry several tags): physiology-mechanism 425, causal 343, universal-deterministic 177, treatment-effect 129, diagnostic 109, numerical 36, recovery-prediction 30, safety-instruction 23, institutional-certification 2.

## Queue separation

Priorities below are tagged by the review discipline they require. **Editorial** items can be assessed for accuracy of wording and attribution without clinical sign-off, but no editorial fix may be applied to scripts in this phase. **Clinical**, **legal**, **accessibility**, and **site** items require the named qualified reviewers; an AI-assisted inventory cannot disposition them.

## P1 themes (review first)

### T1 — Session 20 crisis framing, extended (CLINICAL — blocking relationship)
The Phase 3 gap scan of Session 20 found 7 further material claims around the BLOCKING entries ER-001–004, including the whiteboard "Deconstruction Loop" presented as "the exact mechanism of Law #20," the group "Rebirth Protocol" instruction, and a temporariness affirmation delivered to the group. These are not separate from the existing blockers; they are the passages through which the blocked framing is operationalized. They must be dispositioned together with ER-001–004 by a qualified suicide-prevention clinician. No replacement wording is proposed in this phase.

### T2 — Session 76 and Crisis Package operational safety content (CLINICAL + SITE)
12 gap claims in Session 76 and 4 in the Crisis Package, including: lethal-means instructions (a "10-minute barrier" rationale and a 3-day medication-supply instruction with no prescriber-consultation qualifier); an SPI trigger definition; a 7-day post-incident follow-up obligation (ER-797); and institutional claims that a named screening tool "is used in every VA setting" (ER-793) and that the Session 76 document may not be altered "without written authorization from the VA contract officer" (ER-782). The 23 safety-instruction-tagged entries across the inventory belong to this queue. These are procedures, not just propositions: they require clinical review AND site-implementation review of triggers, role boundaries, and local pathways. The Crisis Package S-REG metric note (presupposing the RGP effectiveness asserted in ER-011) is recorded with ER-011, not as a new claim.

### T3 — The Anchor protocol scripts (CLINICAL)
Three entries — ER-023 (44 locations), ER-278 (27 locations), ER-332 (28 locations) — are scripted breathing/grounding protocols ("In for four… hold… out for six. Do it.") read aloud at session openings across the curriculum, with traffic-light opt-out language in some variants. They are delivered instructions with an implied regulatory effect, recorded at 99 entry-location matches in total (different instructions can appear in the same opening). Review question: what role, optionality, contraindication screening, and escalation boundary apply — the same question PCC-011 poses for the RGP (ER-011), extended to the session-level scripts. General breathing research does not validate these specific scripts.

### T4 — The "one second of air" refrain family (CLINICAL)
This family contains 53 wording entries and 139 entry-location records at 72 distinct literal phrase positions, restating or varying "Naming it gives your prefrontal cortex one second of air." The two-sentence forms were assessed in Phase 2 as ER-005 and ER-014 (narrower affect-labeling evidence; the one-second timing is a metaphor, untested in crisis or panic). Phase 3 shows the refrain is not confined to Sessions 01 and 20: it closes most sessions, in several second-sentence variants, and Session 50 pairs it with "three seconds" in the same sentence — an internal inconsistency a reviewer should see. Disposition of ER-005/ER-014 should explicitly cover or exclude these locations and their individual contexts.

### T5 — The "somatic markers of this law" family (CLINICAL — blocking relationship)
21 entries / 22 locations apply the sentence pattern registered as ER-001 ("…are all somatic markers of this law") to session-specific symptom lists across the curriculum — nausea, rage, shame, dissociation, burnout, and others. ER-001's blocking question (does labeling symptoms as markers of a healing law risk normalizing danger signals?) therefore recurs curriculum-wide, not only in Session 20. A clinician's disposition should state whether it covers the whole family.

### T6 — Diagnostic reclassification claims (CLINICAL)
109 entries carry the diagnostic tag. Recurring forms: "You are reacting to the system mismatch, not just your own symptoms"; "System-Mismatched" as an identity label; symptom lists relabeled as markers, data, or signals. These tell participants what their symptoms are and are not. Misclassification risk runs both directions: a reframe can relieve shame, and it can also redirect someone away from assessment of a condition the reframe does not cover. Requires clinical review; not an editorial wording fix alone.

### T7 — Deterministic permanence claims (CLINICAL)
A subset of the 177 universal-deterministic entries states permanence as biological fact: "biological cost"/"biological reality" closings, Session 43's goal of teaching "the biological reality that self-care was never permitted," "prefrontal cortex never even gets a vote," "voice doesn't go quiet again." These conflict in spirit with the curriculum's own recalibration language (see ER-013's Phase 2 assessment) and risk teaching hopelessness as physiology. Review with the same reviewer pool as T6.

### T8 — Promised treatment effects (CLINICAL)
129 entries carry the treatment-effect tag, including named protocols (Translation Protocols, Parts Audit, Witness Install, New Story Protocol) presented with assured outcomes ("speech does not automatically trigger the next explosion," protocols that "log the steady signal"). No inspected source in any phase evaluates any WRH protocol. Each named protocol needs either outcome evidence or removal of effect language; that is a design decision for clinical review, not copy-editing.

## P2 backlog (mechanism, causal, numerical, predictive)

- **Mechanism chains and loop diagrams** (425 physiology-mechanism and 343 causal tags): whiteboard causal chains in nearly every session (e.g., Session 13's dorsal-vagal chain, Session 44's control-restoration chain). Review as families where wording is identical; variants individually.
- **Numerical claims** (36 entries): durations, percentages, heart-rate and timing figures inside narratives and teaching points (e.g., "heart was at 140 BPM," "in half a second"). Each needs a source or removal of the figure; narrative numbers presented as physiology are in scope, clearly fictional color is not — the register's context notes distinguish them imperfectly and reviewers should expect borderline calls.
- **Recovery predictions** (30 entries): outcome assurances and trajectory claims ("the cycle can be broken, one behavior at a time" used as a closing promise).
- **Unattributed theory**: polyvagal constructs (neuroception, dorsal vagal) recur well beyond ER-010/ER-015's locations, generally without attribution. Attribution is an editorial action; whether the framework may be taught as mechanism is a clinical/scientific question (PCC-010/PCC-015 cover the pattern).
